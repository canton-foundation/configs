## Confidential Annex Process for SV Rights CIPs

Status: Draft for Review

Purpose: This process applies only to CIPs requesting Super Validator rights where specific commitments, milestones, or supporting information cannot reasonably be made public at submission. The CIP itself remains public; only narrowly scoped information may be included in a confidential annex.

### 1. Purpose
Canton Foundation governance is generally intended to be transparent. This process applies only to CIPs requesting Super Validator rights where specific commitments, milestones, or supporting information are subject to a legitimate confidentiality restriction, such as legal, regulatory, contractual, security, or non-public commercial restrictions. For purposes of this process, “SV rights” means Weight, rewards, or other rights or privileges granted through an applicable Super Validator governance vote. This process does not apply to CIPs generally. Confidential treatment must be limited to the minimum information necessary and should not be used merely because public disclosure would be inconvenient.

This process establishes a narrow exception for handling the confidential annex while preserving the existing Super Validator governance process. It establishes:
 - who may review confidential annex information;
 - how a public CIP with a confidential annex proceeds through governance; and
 - when confidential information is expected to become public.

A public CIP is always required for the public governance record. The public CIP should contain as much substantive information as reasonably possible. Confidential information must be separated into an annex rather than making the proposal itself confidential.

### 2. Who May Access Confidential Annex Information

The confidential annex may be provided to:

 - Tokenomics Committee voting members, elected alternates, and the representative from FAAC; (up to 9 people)
 - Super Validator Accountability Committee (SVAC) voting members and elected alternates; (up to 7 people)
 - designated Canton Foundation staff necessary to administer the governance process; and
 - Foundation legal counsel where necessary.

Confidential materials are not distributed through the general Tokenomics mailing list, public GitHub repository, general committee meetings, community forums, or other public governance channels.

The sponsoring or endorsing Super Validator does not automatically receive the confidential materials solely because it is acting as sponsor or endorser.

Access to the confidential annex is for review and administration only. It does not confer final approval authority and does not alter the voting rights or decision-making authority of the Super Validators under the applicable governance process.

### 3. Governance Process for a Public CIP with Confidential Annex

A public CIP accompanied by a confidential annex will proceed as follows:

1. **Tokenomics and SVAC Review**

   The confidential annex is provided to the Tokenomics Committee and SVAC for review. SVAC reviews whether the commitments and milestones are sufficiently clear, measurable, and capable of later verification. Tokenomics reviews the proposal and confidential annex for purposes of making its recommendation. Neither body approves the CIP on behalf of the Super Validators.

2. **Tokenomics Vote**

   Following review, the Tokenomics Committee votes on whether to recommend the public CIP and its associated confidential commitments and milestones.

3. **Foundation Sponsorship**

   If approved by Tokenomics, the Canton Foundation may act as the sponsoring Super Validator for purposes of advancing the CIP through the Super Validator governance process.

4. **Tokenomics Chair Endorsement**

   The Tokenomics Chair, acting in their capacity as a representative of a voting Super Validator, provides the required Super Validator endorsement.

5. **Super Validator Vote**

   The CIP proceeds to the Super Validators for the applicable governance vote. Super Validators remain the final decision-makers under the applicable governance rules. This process does not change voting thresholds or transfer final approval authority to Tokenomics or SVAC; those bodies provide review, verification, and recommendations concerning the confidential annex.

The public CIP must identify that a confidential annex exists, explain at a high level why confidentiality is required, identify the governance bodies that reviewed it, and state their review outcome or recommendation without disclosing protected substance.

## 4. Public CIP and Confidential Annex

Every CIP using this process must have a public version suitable for:

- the public CIP GitHub repository;
- applicable public governance mailing lists; and
- the public governance record.

The public CIP should state:

- the identity of the applicant;
- the overall purpose of the CIP;
- the maximum Weight, reward, or other governance action being requested;
- a meaningful public description of the commitments or milestones to the extent possible, and that additional detail is contained in a confidential annex;
- the general reason confidentiality is required and which governance bodies reviewed the confidential annex; and
- how completion will be verified and what information is expected to become public upon completion.

The confidential annex may contain the specific commitments, milestones, supporting evidence, commercial terms, counterparty information, regulatory materials, or other information that cannot reasonably be disclosed publicly. Confidential treatment should be limited to the minimum information necessary, and the public CIP should not omit the existence or general nature of the commitments being reviewed.

## 5. Disclosure Following Milestone Completion

Milestones may remain confidential while they are pending, but there should be a presumption that the substance and outcome of a milestone become public once it has been completed and the associated reward or Weight is released.

When a milestone is completed, the public governance record should ordinarily be updated to state the milestone or commitment that was completed, the date of completion, the reviewing body’s verification, and any Weight, reward, or other governance consequence released. If the milestone itself cannot yet be described publicly, the update should state that continued confidentiality is required, identify the general reason, and provide a reasonable trigger or review point for later disclosure where possible. This does not require publishing:

- customer names;
- contracts;
- regulatory materials;
- commercial terms;
- underlying evidence; or
- anything that still has a legitimate confidentiality restriction.
